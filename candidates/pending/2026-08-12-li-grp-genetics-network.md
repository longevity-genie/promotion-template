If you work in gene therapy, would you check whether our ratings match what you see in the clinic? The library now covers 136 genes, 751 registered clinical trials and 1,134 experimental records.

The genes come from 72 source species whose adaptations humans do not have. Each claim is rated by where its evidence stops: cell line, model animal, primate, human trial or market. We keep separate ratings for separate claims, and record the delivery method, because a result in a transduced mouse liver is not the same as a result in a transfected cell line.

The failures are recorded alongside the successes. Dsup halves DNA damage in human kidney cells and promotes double-strand breaks in rat cortical neurons. Lin28a improved regeneration in neonatal mice and did nothing in adults. MGMT P140K has reached chemo-protection trials.

Behind it are 1,134 experimental records, 973 DOI-linked references and 109 organisations: 69 labs, 37 biotech companies and 3 clinics. The nine relational tables are open under MIT.

There is a character generator on top with a 100-credit budget. It gets people into the entries; the database is the part I would like this group to audit.
