Sharing an open, citable resource rather than a paper: a curated knowledgebase of 136 genes that let other organisms do things humans cannot, with every claim rated by translational stage and linked to its source. Corrections and gene nominations are welcome, and it is open under MIT.

The curation standard is the part worth describing. Each claim is placed on a ladder, purified protein through cell line, model animal, primate, human trial and market, rather than marked supported or unsupported, and mechanism and intervention are rated separately where confidence in the two differs. Where a result reversed, both results are recorded: tardigrade Dsup halves DNA damage in human kidney cells and promoted double-strand breaks in rat cortical neurons; axolotl Lin28a improved regeneration in neonatal mice and not in adults; a 2023 follow-up failed to reproduce the key elephant TP53 binding result.

Current contents: 136 genes across six categories from 72 source species, 1,134 experimental records, 973 DOI-linked references, 751 registered clinical trials and 109 organisations working on these targets. Nine relational tables, SQLite synced from DoltHub.

It was built for a science-and-art project shown at Milano Design Week and RoBioinfo 2026, so there is an interactive front end over the data, but the database is the citable part and the reason I am posting it here.
