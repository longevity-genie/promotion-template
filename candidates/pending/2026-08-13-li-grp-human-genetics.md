A request for this group: we rate 136 genes by how far their evidence travelled, and I would like geneticists to tell me where we got it wrong. Corrections are the reason to put this online.

The problem we ran into is that a binary works / does not work rating is useless for this material. FOXO3 carries the strongest human longevity association of any gene, and exactly one lab has ever put it in a mouse and measured lifespan, around 30% in Inci 2025, still awaiting replication. That is not works and it is not does not work. It is a specific, nameable gap.

So every claim is placed on a ladder, from purified protein through cell line, model animal, primate, human trial and market. One gene can sit on different rungs for different claims. CAHS D is stronger in yeast than in human cells. Melanin rates higher for passive radiation shielding than for radiosynthesis. Klotho has a dosing window where two copies of KL-VS are worse than one. A 2023 follow-up failed to reproduce the key elephant TP53 binding result.

The current data are 136 genes from 72 source species, 1,134 experimental records and 973 DOI-linked references. The database is open under MIT. Each gene page also has Ask GPT and Ask Claude buttons if you would rather interrogate the sources than read the summary.
