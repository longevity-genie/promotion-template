For anyone here who argues about which longevity interventions are real: we catalogued 136 genes from other species and rated each claim by how far it actually got. I would like the ratings challenged.

The framing that made this worth building is that does it work is the wrong question. FOXO3 carries the strongest human longevity association of any gene, and one lab has put it in a mouse and measured lifespan. Klotho helps at one copy of KL-VS and hurts at two. Elephant multi-copy TP53 answers Peto's paradox and may cost something on the aging side. Every claim sits on a ladder, cell line through model animal, primate, human trial and market, and mechanism and intervention are rated separately.

136 genes from 72 source species, 1,134 experimental records, 973 DOI-linked references and 751 registered clinical trials. Open under MIT.

There is a character generator on top: 100 credits, 106 playable genes, and a budget too small to buy everything. That is what makes people read before they spend. Nickname only, no email, no account.
