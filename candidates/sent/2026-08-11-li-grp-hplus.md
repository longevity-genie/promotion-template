If you have ever argued about human enhancement without a shared list of what is actually on the table, here is one: 109 genes borrowed from other species, each rated by how far the evidence really got. Try it, argue with the ratings, and tell me which ones I got wrong.

It seems obvious to me that advances in science should be used to enhance humans. What I could never find was a shared knowledgebase of what other species could teach us about doing it, something the rest of us could check, correct and extend. So we built one, and then wrapped it in a character generator so people would actually read it.

You get 100 credits and 80 playable genes drawn from 71 source species. Tardigrade Dsup for radiation resistance, naked mole-rat hyaluronic acid for cancer resistance, African elephant multi-copy TP53, axolotl Lin28a for regeneration. The budget is deliberately too small. You cannot afford everything, so you start reading the evidence before you spend.

The part that matters for this group is how we rate things. Not works or does not work, but how far up the translational ladder a claim actually got: cell line, model animal, primate, human trial, market. The same gene carries different ratings for different claims. Dsup halves DNA damage in human kidney cells; in rat cortical neurons it promoted double-strand breaks and killed them. Lin28a improved regeneration in neonatal mice and did nothing in adults. Those reversals are the reason for split ratings, and they are the most interesting entries in the library.

Eight of the therapies in it are already sold to adults today. That number is the honest answer to how far off any of this is.

109 genes, 1,023 experiments, 850 DOI-linked references, open under MIT. A nickname is all you need to play, no email, no account.
