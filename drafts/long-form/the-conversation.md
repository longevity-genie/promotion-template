## Pitch (200 words maximum)

> Proposed headline: What tardigrades, elephants and axolotls could actually lend us - and what they could not
>
> I am an aging researcher at the University of Rostock. Popular coverage of genetic enhancement collapses a long translational ladder into a binary: a gene either gives you a superpower or it does not. The reality is more interesting and almost never reported.
>
> I would write about three cases from a knowledgebase of 1,023 experiments my team catalogued. Tardigrade Dsup does shield DNA from radiation damage, and it also behaved badly in nerve cells - it is not a universal shield. FOXO3 is the strongest longevity association in all of human genetics, and nobody has ever overexpressed it in a mammal and measured lifespan; that experiment has simply never been published. African elephants carry roughly twenty copies of TP53 and their cells commit suicide at more than twice the human rate under DNA damage, and the same redundancy could plausibly accelerate aging in a human.
>
> The argument: what makes enhancement science worth public attention is precisely where the evidence stops, and we lose that when we report it as yes or no.
>
