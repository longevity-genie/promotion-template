## Creative Applications Network

Submit form at `creativeapplications.net/submit-project/`

**Description field:**

> Materialized Enhancements is a browser tool that turns choices about real genes into a 3D-printable sculpture. You get 100 enhancement credits and choose from 106 playable genes drawn from 72 source species - tardigrade Dsup for radiation resistance, naked-mole-rat hyaluronic acid for cancer resistance, elephant TP53 and axolotl Lin28a for regeneration. The budget is too small for an everything build, so people read before they spend. The project premiered at Milano Design Week, where visitors who did not come from biology stayed to inspect the entries.
>
> The generative layer takes the biophysical properties of the selected genes - protein mass, exon count, GRAVY score, intrinsic disorder, isoelectric point, and the size of the biological system each belongs to - and uses them to seed and parameterise an organic Voronoi shell. The shell must slice and print cleanly on low-tolerance machines across a very large combination space with no human in the loop, which shaped the geometry more than any aesthetic decision did. This is procedural generation from biological data; no generative AI is involved anywhere in the pipeline.
>
> Underneath is an open, citable knowledgebase of 136 genes, 1,134 experimental records and 973 DOI-linked references. Each claim is rated by where the evidence stops - purified protein, cell line, model animal, non-human primate, human trial or market - and mechanism confidence is kept separate from intervention confidence. Some entries are deliberately unflattering. The water-holding frog aquaporin is there because the popular enhancement story is not supported by the frog's own gene data.
>
> By Anton Kulaga (aging researcher, University of Rostock), Livia Zaharia (parametric designer), Newton Winter and Markel. Open source.

Man erhält 100 Punkte und wählt aus 106 spielbaren Genen aus 72 Quellspezies - etwa Dsup des Bärtierchens, Hyaluronsäure des Nacktmulls oder die zusätzlichen TP53-Kopien des Afrikanischen Elefanten.
