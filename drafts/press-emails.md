## Creative Applications Network

Submit form at `creativeapplications.net/submit-project/`

**Description field:**

> Materialized Enhancements is a browser tool that turns choices about real genes into a unique, 3D-printable sculpture. You are given 100 enhancement credits and a library of 80 playable genes drawn from 108 organisms - tardigrade Dsup for radiation resistance, naked mole-rat hyaluronic acid for cancer resistance, African elephant multi-copy TP53, axolotl Lin28a for regeneration. The budget is deliberately too small, so every credit is a real choice, and people read the science in order to spend it well. It premiered at Milano Design Week, where non-biologists crowded around it and read the entries on most genes.
>
> The generative layer takes the biophysical properties of the selected genes - protein mass, exon count, GRAVY score, intrinsic disorder, isoelectric point, and the size of the biological system each belongs to - and uses them to seed and parameterise an organic Voronoi shell. The shell must slice and print cleanly on low-tolerance machines across a very large combination space with no human in the loop, which shaped the geometry more than any aesthetic decision did. This is procedural generation from biological data; no generative AI is involved anywhere in the pipeline.
>
> Underneath sits an open, citable knowledgebase of 109 genes and 1,023 catalogued experiments. Every claim is rated by how far up the translational ladder it actually got - purified protein, cell line, model animal, non-human primate, human trial, market - with split ratings where confidence in the mechanism differs from confidence in the intervention. Eight of the therapies in the library are already sold to adults today. Some entries are deliberately unflattering: the water-holding frog aquaporin is included as a case study in how a plausible enhancement story can be assembled entirely from sources that do not support it.
>
> By Anton Kulaga (aging researcher, University of Rostock), Livia Zaharia (parametric designer), Newton Winter and Markel. Open source.
